The economics of enterprise AI

AI’s cost curve is bending — the wrong way for insurance.

Per-token prices have fallen ~1,000× in three years. But the workloads insurers actually want — multi-step agents that reason across policies, claims, and coverage — are moving in the opposite direction. This is a brief on why, on what one top-5 carrier is doing about it, and on what architecture that answer has a name.

CNBC Squawk Box · 1 Jul 2026 · 4:12
“Something has gone completely wrong with how AI is sold.”
Alex Karp, CEO, Palantir — on enterprises “paying for tokens that create no value” while surrendering their “weights and alpha.”
▶ Watch on CNBC →
01 / The paradox

Per-token prices are falling. Per-query costs are exploding.

The headline story is real. Andreessen Horowitz’s widely-cited “LLMflation” note pegs the fall at roughly ten-fold per year: ~1,000× over three years for equal-quality inference. Frontier list prices moved the same direction. GPT-5 launched in August 2025 at $1.25 / $10 per million tokens — less than half the launch price of GPT-4o a year earlier.

But that is the wrong number for the workloads that matter.

The reasoning and agentic paradigms that arrived in 2024 — OpenAI’s o-series, Claude extended thinking, Gemini Deep Think — consume 8 to 20× more output tokens per task than their predecessors, and every one of those tokens is billed at the (expensive) output rate. Artificial Analysis stopped charting reasoning models on per-token axes because the comparison no longer means anything at task level.

BCG’s Widening AI Value Gap (September 2025) uses an insurance claim as its worked example: “An enterprise-grade agent that processes an insurance claim by reasoning through the steps, looking up policy details and calling specialized sub-agents can use five to 30 times more tokens to complete the task.” Enterprises already past 10 billion tokens a month are projected to cross 100 billion by 2028. Multiply either number by the volume of a US P&C book — the industry crossed $1 trillion in direct premiums written in 2024 — and the inference-cost line item becomes a P&L item.

And the market has started to notice

MIT’s Project NANDA report in July 2025 — the widely-cited “95% of enterprise GenAI pilots produce zero return” study — drew a real methodology debate but is corroborated by independent surveys: Gartner (30% of PoCs abandoned by end-2025, >40% of agentic projects canceled by 2027), McKinsey (88% of firms adopt AI; only 6% see >5% EBIT impact).

Goldman Sachs’ head of global equity research Jim Covello put the skeptical case plainly: “AI technology is exceptionally expensive, and to justify those costs, the technology must be able to solve complex problems, which it isn’t designed to do.”

The wins are all at the linguistic boundary.

Submission ingest. Claims triage. First-notice-of-loss automation. Customer-message generation. Document extraction from ACORD forms and loss runs. Every one of these workloads shares the same shape: the input is unstructured natural language, and the output is a structured decision or a fluent reply. That is exactly the surface area where an LLM earns its cost — where language is genuinely irreducible.

The workloads where LLM-only economics fall apart are the opposite shape: multi-step reasoning across policies, coverages, appetite rules, treaty terms, and portfolio position. That reasoning is structured. It should not need to be re-derived from prose on every request. That is where enterprises are paying “for tokens that create no value.”

03 / The proof point

AIG built an ontology of four million data points — and put a Lloyd’s syndicate under it.

In December 2025, AIG announced Syndicate 2479 at Lloyd’s — a $300M special-purpose syndicate with Amwins and Blackstone, launched underwriting on 1 January 2026. In March 2026, the same architecture was extended to a strategic collaboration with McGill and Partners for up to $1.6B in specialty gross written premium. Both programs run on the same platform: an AIG-built ontology of “more than four million industry data points,” wired into Palantir Foundry, with Anthropic’s Claude family reading and writing at the edges.

This is the ontology-first architecture at production scale, inside a top-5 P&C carrier. AIG did not point an LLM at a policy repository and hope. They constructed the structured layer — risks, exposures, coverages, decisions, syndicate appetite — and gave the LLMs constrained access to it.

It is not often that a top-5 P&C CEO shares a stage with the CEOs of his ontology vendor and his foundation-model vendor to explain that this is his 2030 growth engine. It happened.

Karp’s framing of the same architecture, from Palantir’s Q4 2025 shareholder letter (February 2026), reads as the argument in the abstract:

“Palantir is alone in choosing to exclusively focus on scaling the operational leverage made possible by the rapid advancements of AI models, a trend that we first called ‘commodity cognition’ well before others started repeating it.”
Alex Karp · Palantir Q4 2025 Letter to Shareholders
And AIG is not a one-off

Swiss Re runs more than 200 projects and 6+ petabytes of data on Foundry, with one-in-three employees active users. SOMPO Holdings runs more than 10,000 agents on Palantir for dynamic pricing, quoting, and risk flagging. Palantir was named a Leader in the Forrester Wave: AI/ML Platforms, Q3 2024, on the strength of “decision-centric Ontology.” The pattern is converging.

04 / The architecture has a name

What Karp calls the application layer, the field calls neurosymbolic.

The academic name for the pattern is neurosymbolic AI: a symbolic core — ontology, rules, explicit reasoning — with neural models used at the edges where language and pattern-matching are irreducible. It is not new. What is new is the industrial evidence.

Every serious reasoning benchmark being won in 2024–2025 was won by a hybrid, not by a pure LLM. DeepMind’s AlphaGeometry solved 25 of 30 IMO-level geometry problems by pairing a language model with a symbolic deduction engine (Nature, January 2024). AlphaProof + AlphaGeometry 2 hit IMO silver-medal level in 2024 by handing formalized problems to a Lean symbolic engine that solved one within nineteen seconds. DeepMind’s FunSearch discovered new mathematical results on the cap-set and bin-packing problems by combining an LLM with an automated evaluator (Nature, December 2023). Every one of these is a symbolic engine with an LLM in the loop, not the reverse.

The independent voices making the case in public are named and specific. Gary Marcus: “The biggest advance since the LLM is neurosymbolic.” François Chollet, on the o3 result: it “comes at a steep cost, and wouldn’t quite be economical yet — you could pay a human to solve ARC-AGI tasks for roughly $5 per task.” Yann LeCun on pure scaling: a dead end. Stephen Wolfram on ChatGPT + Wolfram|Alpha as the first large-scale statistical + symbolic system.

The analyst class has caught up. Gartner’s 2025 Hype Cycle for AI explicitly names Neurosymbolic AI and Composite AI as emerging paradigms for regulated, high-stakes decisions.

Honest gap flagged: no public source states “insurance NSAI is X% cheaper than LLM-only in production.” The insurance-specific cost math is a composition of three cited principles — the o3 cost curve, symbolic execution at near-zero marginal cost, and hybrid systems’ benchmark efficiency — not a single vendor headline number.

05 / The payoff

You don’t have to rent tokens forever.

Here is the argument, condensed. Per-token deflation does not solve the cost problem because the workloads that create value are exactly the ones whose token count is exploding. Enterprises that put an LLM at the center of a decision workflow surrender two things at once: money to the model vendor, and structural leverage over their own reasoning.

The alternative — the one AIG chose, the one Karp has articulated in the loudest public form, the one DeepMind and Wolfram have proved out on the hardest benchmarks — is to invert the architecture. Put the ontology and the rules at the center. Invoke the LLM only at the linguistic boundary, where language is irreducible. Pay for tokens once, on the way in and on the way out. Compound the value of the ontology over years, on your balance sheet, not the model vendor’s.

That is what neurosymbolic AI is. It is what NSAI stands for. And, as of the first quarter of 2026, it is the architecture that a top-5 P&C carrier is running a live Lloyd’s syndicate on.
