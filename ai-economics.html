The architecture of the moat

The moat isn’t the model. It’s the ontology.

In January 2026 AIG began underwriting a live Lloyd’s syndicate on top of an ontology it built itself — four million industry data points, wired into Palantir Foundry, with Anthropic’s Claude at the linguistic edges. It is the clearest public bet a top-5 P&C carrier has made on the shape of the next decade of insurance AI. This is a brief on what they chose, why Alex Karp keeps making the same argument on television, and what the pattern is called in the academic literature.

Alex Karp · CNBC Squawk Box · Jul 2026 Open on YouTube →
01 / The proof point

AIG built an ontology of four million data points — and put a Lloyd’s syndicate under it.

In December 2025, AIG, Amwins, and Blackstone announced Syndicate 2479 at Lloyd’s: a $300M special-purpose syndicate managed by Talbot that began underwriting on 1 January 2026. In March 2026 the same architecture was extended to a strategic collaboration with McGill and Partners for up to $1.6 billion in specialty gross written premium. Both programs run on the same platform: an AIG-built ontology of “more than four million industry data points,” wired into Palantir Foundry, with Anthropic’s Claude family reading and writing at the edges.

This is the ontology-first architecture, at production scale, inside a top-5 P&C carrier. AIG did not point an LLM at a policy repository and hope. They constructed the structured layer — risks, exposures, coverages, decisions, syndicate appetite — and gave the LLMs constrained access to it. The distinction sounds academic until you look at what it produces.

On the Q4 2025 earnings call in February 2026, CEO Peter Zaffino framed the outcome as a step-change, not a marginal improvement:

“We’re seeing a massive shift in our ability to process a significant submission flow way beyond our expectations without additional human capital resources. The acceleration and the opportunity is greater than I thought at Investor Day.”
Peter Zaffino · CEO, AIG · Q4 2025 earnings call, February 2026

At AIG’s Investor Day in April 2025, Zaffino brought both Alex Karp of Palantir and Dario Amodei of Anthropic onto the stage, moderated by CNBC’s Sara Eisen, to walk through the architecture together. It is not often that a top-5 P&C CEO shares a stage with the CEOs of both his ontology vendor and his foundation-model vendor to explain that this is his 2030 growth engine. That happened.

02 / The thesis

Karp keeps saying the same thing. The moat is the application layer, not the model.

The clip above is the sharpest recent version of an argument Karp has been making publicly for more than a year. The Palantir Q4 2025 shareholder letter (2 February 2026) gives it a name:

“Palantir is alone in choosing to exclusively focus on scaling the operational leverage made possible by the rapid advancements of AI models — a trend that we first called ‘commodity cognition’ well before others started repeating it.”
Alex Karp · Palantir Q4 2025 Letter to Shareholders

The load-bearing word is “commodity.” The thesis, stated plainly: foundation models are converging in capability and collapsing in price; the durable operational leverage — the moat — accrues to whoever owns the ontology and the workflow layer that the models operate on. Rent the tokens; own the ontology.

This is not a lone position. It is the choice a growing set of the industry’s largest carriers has already made. Swiss Re runs more than 200 projects and over six petabytes of data on Palantir Foundry, with roughly one-in-three employees active users. SOMPO Holdings runs more than 10,000 agents on Palantir for dynamic pricing, quoting, and risk flagging. Palantir was named a Leader in the Forrester Wave: AI/ML Platforms, Q3 2024, on the strength of “decision-centric Ontology.” AIG’s bet sits on top of a pattern that is already converging.

03 / What the pattern is called

What Karp calls the application layer, the field calls neurosymbolic AI.

The academic name for the pattern is neurosymbolic AI: a symbolic core — ontology, rules, explicit reasoning — with neural models used at the edges where language and pattern-matching are irreducible. The idea predates the LLM era. What is new is that every hard-reasoning benchmark being won right now is being won by a hybrid, not by a pure model.

DeepMind’s AlphaGeometry solved 25 of 30 IMO-level geometry problems by pairing a language model with a symbolic deduction engine (Nature, January 2024). AlphaProof + AlphaGeometry 2 hit IMO silver-medal level in 2024 by handing formalized problems to a Lean symbolic engine that solved one within nineteen seconds. DeepMind’s FunSearch discovered new mathematical results on the cap-set and bin-packing problems by combining an LLM with an automated evaluator (Nature, December 2023). Every one of these is a symbolic engine with an LLM in the loop, not the other way around.

The independent voices are named and specific. Gary Marcus: “The biggest advance since the LLM is neurosymbolic.” François Chollet, on the o3 result: it “comes at a steep cost, and wouldn’t quite be economical yet — you could pay a human to solve ARC-AGI tasks for roughly $5 per task.” Yann LeCun, on pure scaling: a dead end. Stephen Wolfram, on the ChatGPT + Wolfram|Alpha combination: the first large-scale statistical + symbolic AI system.

The analyst class has caught up. Gartner’s 2025 Hype Cycle for AI explicitly names Neurosymbolic AI and Composite AI as emerging paradigms for regulated, high-stakes decisions. Insurance is the archetypal case.

04 / A tailwind

The economics point the same way.

One quiet detail worth noting. Per-token prices are falling, but per-query costs for the multi-step reasoning and agentic workflows that insurers actually want have been moving the opposite direction — reasoning models consume many times more output tokens per task, each billed at the output rate. BCG’s Widening AI Value Gap (September 2025) makes the point using an insurance claim as its example: an enterprise-grade agent processing a single claim “can use five to 30 times more tokens” than a single-shot LLM call.

An ontology-first architecture avoids that compounding by design. Rules and deterministic reasoning run at near-zero marginal cost; the LLM is invoked at the edges — once on ingest, once on output. It is not why AIG built what they built, but it is why the choice holds up as volume grows.

05 / The payoff

Own the ontology. Rent the tokens.

Here is the argument, condensed. A top-5 carrier has publicly bet its 2030 growth engine on an ontology-first architecture. The industry’s most vocal enterprise-software CEO argues, on quarterly television, that this is the only architecture in which value compounds rather than leaks upstream. The academic literature has a name for the pattern and the analyst class has begun to use it. And the economics of the alternative are getting worse, not better, at exactly the workloads where insurers most want AI to work.

Neurosymbolic AI is what that architecture is called. NSAI is the shorthand. And as of the first quarter of 2026, it is the architecture that a top-5 P&C carrier is running a live Lloyd’s syndicate on.
