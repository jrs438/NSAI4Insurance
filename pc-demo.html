Observed Pain Points

Fast, frictionless, optimized for conversion — not for accuracy

The customer’s self-classification is the single source of truth. If “handyman” hides plumbing, roof-surface work, or any height exposure, the wrong class is bound, the wrong rate is applied, and the customer’s coverage has gaps that only surface at claim time. Industry research estimates this misclassification produces billions in premium leakage across the small commercial segment.

  • Self-classification is unverified
  • No decomposition of described activities
  • Speed optimizes conversion, not accuracy
  • Coverage gaps surface at claim, not at bind

The realistic LLM-fronted deployment today: the appetite guide, rate manual, and coverage defaults are indexed for retrieval. The LLM is competent and conversion-optimized — the failure here is structural, not stupid.

What RAG got right — and what it missed

Competent, fluent, conversion-optimized — and pricing the wrong risk profile.

What RAG got right:

  • Correctly identified the business as a small handyman operation
  • Found the relevant appetite and rate references
  • Produced a professional, fluent quote ready to bind

What RAG missed:

  • The leak patching and shingle replacement is roof-surface work — a different carrier activity class with height/fall exposure that falls outside standard DTC handyman appetite
  • The water heater work involves plumbing exposure (water damage to landlord property) that the standard handyman BOP doesn’t fully address
  • The customer’s coverage has gaps that will only surface at claim time
  • The premium is competitive — but it’s competitive because it’s pricing the wrong risk profile

Hi Marcos — thanks for the details on Delgado Home Services. Before we bind, we want to make sure your coverage actually matches the work you do.

Based on what you described, your business is a bit more than a standard handyman operation:

  • · Most of your work is light interior repair — that fits our handyman program.
  • · The water-heater work creates water-damage exposure at landlord properties, so we would add terms designed for that exposure.
  • · The leak patching and shingle replacement is roof-surface work. Even if you only do it occasionally, it carries height and fall exposure that standard handyman coverage does not contemplate.

We have two ways to handle this:

Option A · Full-scope quote for everything you described

Estimated annual premium: $4,100. This route includes the roof-surface work you described, subject to specialty underwriting terms and final review. It usually takes about 5 business days because roof work needs an underwriter to confirm the details.

Option B · Bind today as handyman + water-heater work, with roof work excluded

Annual premium: $1,950. This covers your light interior repair and water-heater-related work under the terms shown, but roof-surface work would be excluded. If you continue doing roof work, you should use Option A or arrange separate coverage for that work.

This quote covers liability and tools/equipment. It does not cover the truck itself or auto liability; we can route that separately if needed.

Also, because you mentioned one helper, we should separately check whether you need workers’ compensation coverage. That is not included in the GL/tools quote.

These options assume the work mix you described; we’ll confirm the exact split before bind.

Most repair businesses that continue doing roof work prefer Option A, because they do not want surprise gaps. But if you rarely do roof work and want to bind today, Option B is available too. Want us to walk through either option?

Industry context

Verisk research estimates that small commercial business misclassification produces up to $6.5 billion in first-year premium leakage and more than $22 billion over four years in BOP premium alone. The issue isn’t that customers lie — it’s that “handyman” or “landscaper” or “warehouse” can hide exposures that don’t fit the self-classification. A DTC flow that takes the self-description at face value is the leakage.
