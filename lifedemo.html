01 / Where the value sits in L&A

What’s at stake.
Precisely.

Before we talk about how NSAI helps, let’s be specific about what’s at stake. At a typical $15–20B life and annuity carrier, the following categories of value are recurring, quantifiable, and currently undercaptured.

01Reinvestment timing

Maturities roll into a market that has moved. New money goes to work weeks later than it could, and on a $20B general account, even 30bps of reinvestment lag costs roughly $6M annually per percent of book turning over. At normal turnover rates of 8–12% per year, the recurring annual cost sits in the $10–20M range.

02Spread dislocation capture

When spreads widen, carriers with sufficient liquidity and authorization buy. Carriers running quarterly approval cycles miss the window. The differential is permanent NII for the next decade on every dollar deployed. A two-week cycle delay during a 50bps dislocation event costs roughly $5–10M of locked-in annual spread on a $300M deployment sleeve, compounding forward for the life of the position.

03Liability repricing signals

Lapse signals are pricing signals. Elevated surrenders in a fixed annuity book are not just a liquidity event — they are a segmentation signal. Hot-money policyholders behave differently from relationship policyholders; treating them identically costs margin. A carrier that reprices crediting-rate tiers in response to lapse cohort data captures $5–8M annually on a typical fixed annuity book relative to one running a uniform repricing calendar.

04Capital velocity

Reinsurance evaluations that take six months miss favorable pricing windows. Capital trapped in legacy in-force blocks for an extra two quarters is capital not redeploying at hurdle rates. On a $4B legacy block, two quarters of delay on a 50% cession represents approximately $35M of foregone redeployment income — capital present, but idle, while the market moves.

05PRT capital efficiency

Pension risk transfer is structurally attractive — long-duration assets that fit GA portfolios, limited competition at the top, and mortality improvement that accrues to the carrier. PRT does consume capital: C2 mortality charges and C3 interest rate charges both apply. But PRT generates among the highest NII per dollar of RBC deployed in the product mix. The opportunity is not to avoid capital consumption — it is to win more tenders at better prices within existing capital appetite. A 10-percentage-point improvement in win rate is worth $8–15M annually for an active writer, fully utilizing existing capital rather than requiring incremental allocation.

06Continuous cross-entity portfolio optimization

Multi-entity carriers — a U.S. operating company alongside a Bermuda affiliate or captive — have processes for entity routing, but those processes were built for a quarterly cadence and a static capital snapshot. The value is not in making a routing decision once at transaction time. It is in continuously monitoring capital efficiency across entities as positions evolve, spreads shift, and regulatory capital requirements update — and surfacing rebalancing moves as a live feed of optimization signals. Most carriers have a quarterly process for this. NSAI runs it continuously, across all live positions simultaneously. At a multi-entity $20B carrier, static quarterly routing creates $5–15M of annual ROE drag. That analysis is now always running.

02 / What carriers already have

Sophisticated tools.
Sophisticated teams.

Most large life and annuity carriers do not lack capital management sophistication. This is not a market of unsophisticated actors waiting to be taught ALM.

ActuarialProphet, AXIS, MG-ALFA, Polysystems, RAFM, GGY-AXIS — often multiple in parallel, heavily customized over decades to match the specific liability structure of the company.
Asset managementAladdin, BlackRock Solutions, or proprietary systems managing duration targets, sector allocation, and daily liquidity across a general account spanning dozens of asset classes.
Capital modelingCustom internal RBC and economic capital tools, sometimes built over fifteen years by teams that know the book better than any vendor does.
ALM functionDedicated, often led by senior actuaries with 20+ years of experience and deep familiarity with the specific liability profile of the company.
GovernanceBoard-level capital management committees with independent actuarial review, rating agency relationships, and regulatory reporting infrastructure already in place.

These are not gaps. Anyone arguing that large L&A carriers need to be taught capital management is not a credible counterparty — and will be recognized as such immediately.

But the question is not whether carriers have capability. The question is whether that capability is integrated, fast, and auditable enough to capture the value identified in Section 02.

04 / How NSAI reasons

Not retrieval. Not generation.
Derivation.

Every NSAI recommendation traces to a specific fact and a specific rule. The engine doesn’t summarize documents or generate plausible-sounding answers. It loads the carrier’s world as a typed ontology — blocks, crediting rate tiers, RBC charge components, treaty structures — then evaluates that world against executable constraint rules. The same inputs always produce the same output, with a full proof trace as the byproduct.
